\chapter{Algorithm 2: Deflected Subgradient Method}
\label{chap:dsm}

In this chapter, we apply the deflected subgradient method with Polyak step and with target level (SGPTL) to the ELM LASSO problem.

% ------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Why the plain subgradient method is inadequate}
\label{sec:dsm_motivation}

The ELM LASSO problem~\eqref{eq:original_problem} is nonsmooth due to the $\ell_1$ penalty. The plain subgradient method converges on convex nonsmooth problems but has two structural limitations on ELM LASSO. First, the update $\mathbf{w}_{i+1} = \mathbf{w}_i - \alpha_i \mathbf{g}_i$ has no memory \cite[Slide 10]{frangioni-slides-nonsmooth} and zig-zags on ill-conditioned $\mathbf{X}^T\mathbf{X}$; while it attains the optimal iteration complexity $\Omega(L^2/\varepsilon^2)$ \cite[Thm.~3.2]{bubeck2015convex}\footnote{From the Polyak bound $\bar f_k - f^* \le L\,\|\mathbf{w}_0 - \mathbf{w}^*\|/\sqrt{k}$ in \cite[Thm.~3.2]{bubeck2015convex} one gets $k = O(L^2\|\mathbf{w}_0-\mathbf{w}^*\|^2/\varepsilon^2)$, which we write as $O(L^2/\varepsilon^2)$ absorbing the initial radius. The form $O(L/\varepsilon^2)$ that appears in some sources is the same order after rescaling $\varepsilon$ by $L$.}, the constant $L$ depends on $\|\mathbf{X}\|_2$ and the sublevel-set radius. Second, a pre-set diminishing--square-summable schedule $\sum_i \alpha_i = \infty$, $\sum_i \alpha_i^2 < \infty$ \cite[Slide 10]{frangioni-slides-nonsmooth} forces the step to vanish regardless of the actual gap to $f^*$, so practical progress stalls. We address the first issue with deflection, which injects memory into the search direction, and the second with an adaptive Polyak target-level stepsize in place of a fixed schedule (Section~\ref{sec:dsm_target_level}).

% ------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{The deflection mechanism}
\label{sec:dsm_deflection}

Deflected subgradient methods \cite[Slide 15]{frangioni-slides-nonsmooth} replace the raw subgradient with a direction that blends the current subgradient with the previous search direction:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:deflected_dir}
  \mathbf{d}_i = \gamma_i\, \mathbf{g}_i + (1 - \gamma_i)\, \mathbf{d}_{i-1} \quad (i \ge 1), \qquad \mathbf{d}_0 = \mathbf{g}_0,
\end{equation}
where $\gamma_i \in (0,1]$ is the deflection parameter. The update is $\mathbf{w}_{i+1} = \mathbf{w}_i - \alpha_i\, \mathbf{d}_i$; $\gamma_i = 1$ recovers plain subgradient, $\gamma_i < 1$ damps the zig-zag. The method specifies $\gamma_i$ as the minimiser of $\norm{\mathbf{d}_i}^2$ on $[0,1]$ \cite[Slide 15]{frangioni-slides-nonsmooth}, which maximises the Polyak step $\alpha_i$ since $\norm{\mathbf{d}_i}^2$ is its denominator.

% ------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Convergence framework: balancing deflection and stepsize}
\label{sec:dsm_convergence_conditions}

We use the stepsize-restricted Polyak rule \cite[Slide 15]{frangioni-slides-nonsmooth}:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sr_step}
\alpha_i = \frac{\beta_i\,(f(\mathbf{w}_i) - f^*)}{\norm{\mathbf{d}_i}_2^2}, \quad \beta_i \leq \gamma_i,
\end{equation}
with $\beta_i = \min(\beta,\gamma_i)$ and $\beta = 1$ \cite[Slide 14]{frangioni-slides-nonsmooth}. Since $f^*$ is unknown, we substitute the target level $f_{\mathrm{ref}} - \delta$ (Section~\ref{sec:dsm_target_level}).

\paragraph{Deflection floor $\gamma_{\min} > 0$.}
Condition~(3.5) of~\cite{dantonio2009deflected} requires (when $\lambda_k \ge 0$) the uniform bound $\beta_k \ge \beta^* > 0$ on the stepsize multiplier --- in our notation $\beta_i \ge \beta^*$ for all $i$, not just pointwise positivity. Since $\beta_i = \min(1, \gamma_i)$, a uniform $\gamma_i \ge \gamma_{\min} \in (0,1]$ supplies $\beta_i \ge \gamma_{\min}$, i.e.~$\beta^* = \gamma_{\min}$. The closed-form greedy~\eqref{eq:gamma_opt} gives $\gamma_i \in (0,1]$ at each step but does not bound the sequence away from zero: when consecutive subgradients align near stationarity, the numerator $\norm{\mathbf{d}_{i-1}}_2^2 - \inner{\mathbf{g}_i}{\mathbf{d}_{i-1}}$ of $\gamma^{*}$ in~\eqref{eq:gamma_star} tends to $0^+$ and $\gamma_i$ decays, dragging $\beta_i$ and the Polyak step to zero --- the iterate freezes. Projecting onto $[\gamma_{\min}, 1]$ with $\gamma_{\min} = 0.05$ supplies the missing uniform bound. A sweep $\gamma_{\min} \in \{0.05, 0.1, 0.2, 0.5\}$ on the convergence instance gives record gaps in $[10^{-7}, 10^{-5}]$, with $\gamma_{\min} = 0.05$ marginally best (Section~\ref{sec:setup}).

% ------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Optimal deflection parameter}
\label{sec:dsm_optimal_gamma}

The argmin problem
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gamma_opt}
\gamma_i \in \argmin_{\gamma \in [\gamma_{\min},1]} \norm{\gamma\, \mathbf{g}_i + (1-\gamma)\, \mathbf{d}_{i-1}}_2^2
\end{equation}
is quadratic in $\gamma$; expanding $\norm{\gamma\mathbf{g}_i + (1-\gamma)\mathbf{d}_{i-1}}^2$ by bilinearity and taking the vertex of the resulting upward parabola gives the closed form (annotated as such in \cite[Slide 15]{frangioni-slides-nonsmooth}):
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gamma_star}
\gamma^* = \frac{\norm{\mathbf{d}_{i-1}}_2^2 - \inner{\mathbf{g}_i}{\mathbf{d}_{i-1}}}{\norm{\mathbf{g}_i - \mathbf{d}_{i-1}}_2^2}, \quad \gamma_i = \min(1,\max(\gamma_{\min},\gamma^*)).
\end{equation}
The clip onto $[\gamma_{\min}, 1]$ instead of $[0, 1]$ is our addition (Section~\ref{sec:dsm_convergence_conditions}); the degenerate case $\mathbf{g}_i = \mathbf{d}_{i-1}$ has vanishing leading coefficient and gives $\mathbf{d}_i = \mathbf{d}_{i-1}$ for any $\gamma$, so we set $\gamma_i = 1$ by convention. The first iteration is handled analogously: with $\mathbf{d}_{-1} = \mathbf{0}$ the formula~\eqref{eq:gamma_star} returns $\gamma^* = 0$, which would make $\mathbf{d}_0 = \mathbf{0}$; we set $\gamma_0 = 1$ so that $\mathbf{d}_0 = \mathbf{g}_0$, the plain subgradient first step. In the zig-zag regime $\mathbf{g}_i \approx -\mathbf{d}_{i-1}$ with $\norm{\mathbf{g}_i} \approx \norm{\mathbf{d}_{i-1}}$, the formula gives $\gamma^* \approx 1/2$ and the oscillatory component of $\mathbf{d}_i$ cancels.

\section{The target-level mechanism}
\label{sec:dsm_target_level}

The Polyak stepsize \cite[Slide 12]{frangioni-slides-nonsmooth} requires $f^*$. The fundamental inequality \cite[Slide 10]{frangioni-slides-nonsmooth} is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fundamental}
\norm{\mathbf{w}_{i+1}-\mathbf{w}^*}_2^2 \leq \norm{\mathbf{w}_i-\mathbf{w}^*}_2^2 - 2\alpha_i(f(\mathbf{w}_i)-f^*) + (\alpha_i)^2\norm{\mathbf{g}_i}_2^2,
\end{equation}
minimized at $\alpha_i^* = (f(\mathbf{w}_i)-f^*)/\norm{\mathbf{g}_i}_2^2$ \cite[Slide 12]{frangioni-slides-nonsmooth}. SGPTL replaces $f^*$ with the target level $f_{\mathrm{ref}} - \delta$ \cite[Slide 14]{frangioni-slides-nonsmooth}: $f_{\mathrm{ref}}$ is the best value found so far, $\delta > 0$ an estimate of the residual gap.

A failure mode of this substitution is $f_{\mathrm{ref}} - \delta < f^*$, in which case $f(\mathbf{w}_{i+1}) \ge f^* > f_{\mathrm{ref}} - \delta/2$ for every iterate and the sufficient-descent test $f(\mathbf{w}_{i+1}) \le f_{\mathrm{ref}} - \delta/2$ is unsatisfiable: $f_{\mathrm{ref}}$ never updates and the algorithm silently stalls. The mechanism guards against this through an internal patience counter $r_i = \sum \alpha_j \norm{\mathbf{d}_j}$, the displacement accumulated since the last $f_{\mathrm{ref}}$ update. When $r_i$ exceeds a threshold $R$ without sufficient descent firing, $\delta$ is contracted to $\rho\delta$ with $\rho \in (0,1)$ and $r$ is reset. Repeated contractions drive $\delta \to 0$, eventually restoring $f_{\mathrm{ref}} - \delta \ge f^*$ and unblocking the sufficient-descent test.

\section{The complete algorithm}
\label{sec:dsm_algorithm}

Algorithm~\ref{alg:dsm} presents the full Deflected Subgradient Method with target level for ELM LASSO.

\begin{algorithm}[htbp]
\caption{Deflected Subgradient with Target Level (SGPTL) for ELM LASSO~\cite[Slide 15]{frangioni-slides-nonsmooth}}
\label{alg:dsm}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require $f$ (ELM LASSO objective);\; $i_{\max}$;\; $\beta \in (0,1]$;\; $\delta_0 > 0$;\; $R > 0$;\; $\rho \in (0,1)$;\; $\gamma_{\min} \in (0,1]$
\State $\mathbf{w}_0 \gets \mathbf{0}$ \hfill\emph{(cold start by default; warm start $(\mathbf{X}^T\mathbf{X})^{-1}\mathbf{X}^T\mathbf{y}$ is admissible — see \S\ref{sec:dsm_properties})}
\State $r \gets 0$;\; $\delta \gets \delta_0$;\; $f_{\mathrm{ref}} \gets \bar{f} \gets f(\mathbf{w}_0)$
\For{$i = 0, 1, \ldots, i_{\max}$}
\State Compute $\mathbf{g} \in \partial f(\mathbf{w}_i)$ \hfill\emph{(subgradient of LASSO)}
\If{$i = 0$} \hfill\emph{(no $\mathbf{d}_{-1}$ available)}
\State $\gamma_i \gets 1$;\; $\mathbf{d}_i \gets \mathbf{g}$
\Else
\State Compute $\gamma_i$ via \eqref{eq:gamma_star} \hfill\emph{(optimal deflection, clipped to $[\gamma_{\min},1]$)}
\State $\mathbf{d}_i \gets \gamma_i\, \mathbf{g} + (1-\gamma_i)\, \mathbf{d}_{i-1}$ \hfill\emph{(deflected direction)}
\EndIf
\State $\beta_i \gets \min(\beta,\gamma_i)$ \hfill\emph{(stepsize-restricted, $\beta=1$ default; see \S\ref{sec:dsm_convergence_conditions})}
\State $\alpha_i \gets \beta_i\,(f(\mathbf{w}_i) - (f_{\mathrm{ref}}-\delta))\,/\,\norm{\mathbf{d}_i}_2^2$ \hfill\emph{(Polyak step)}
\State $\mathbf{w}_{i+1} \gets \mathbf{w}_i - \alpha_i\, \mathbf{d}_i$ \hfill\emph{(update)}
\State $\bar{f} \gets \min(\bar{f},\; f(\mathbf{w}_{i+1}))$ \hfill\emph{(update record)}
\If{$f(\mathbf{w}_{i+1}) \leq f_{\mathrm{ref}} - \delta/2$} \hfill\emph{(sufficient descent)}
\State $f_{\mathrm{ref}} \gets \bar{f}$;\; $r \gets 0$
\ElsIf{$r > R$} \hfill\emph{(travel-distance patience exhausted)}
\State $\delta \gets \delta\,\rho$;\; $r \gets 0$
\Else
\State $r \gets r + \alpha_i\norm{\mathbf{d}_i}_2$
\EndIf
\EndFor
\State \Return $\mathbf{w}_{\mathrm{best}}$ (iterate achieving $\bar{f}$)
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\paragraph{Numerical safeguards.}
Three pure floating-point guards lie outside the pseudocode. \emph{(i)}~If $\norm{\mathbf{d}_i}^2 < 10^{-30}$ we terminate. \emph{(ii)}~If $f(\mathbf{w}_i) - (f_{\mathrm{ref}}-\delta) \le 0$ we set $\alpha_i = 0$ (condition~(3.5) of~\cite{dantonio2009deflected}) and trigger the sufficient-descent update at $\mathbf{w}_i$, since $f(\mathbf{w}_i) \le f_{\mathrm{ref}}-\delta/2$. \emph{(iii)}~If $\mathbf{w}_{i+1}$ has a non-finite component we skip the iteration without touching $(\delta, f_{\mathrm{ref}}, r)$.

\subsection{Parameter calibration for ELM LASSO}
\label{sec:dsm_parameters}

SGPTL has five parameters: $\beta$, $\gamma_{\min}$, $\delta_0$, $\rho$, $R$. Each is discussed below with the choice we adopt for ELM~LASSO and its rationale. 

\paragraph{Cold vs.\ warm start.}
Theorem~\ref{thm:dsm_convergence} places no condition on $\mathbf{w}_0$, so we keep the algorithm's default cold start $\mathbf{w}_0 = \mathbf{0}$ and additionally support the OLS warm start $(\mathbf{X}^\top\mathbf{X})^{-1}\mathbf{X}^\top\mathbf{y}$, which lets us run IRLS and SGPTL from the same point when comparing them (Chapter~\ref{chap:comparison}). Measured against an independent reference $f^*$, the cold start is the one that genuinely optimizes, closing most of the initial gap on every instance; the warm start ends lower only on California-ELM, and there because $f(\mathbf{w}_{\mathrm{OLS}})$ already sits close to $f^*$, leaving SGPTL no room to descend, rather than because the warm start aids the method. Section~\ref{sec:warm_cold} reports the figures and per-instance gaps.

\paragraph{$\beta = 1$.}
\cite[(3.1)]{dantonio2009deflected} admits $\beta_k \in (0, 1]$. Substituting the Polyak step $\alpha_i = \beta\,(f(\mathbf{w}_i)-f^*)/\norm{\mathbf{g}_i}_2^2$ into the fundamental inequality~\eqref{eq:fundamental} gives a per-iteration reduction in $\norm{\mathbf{w}_i - \mathbf{w}^*}_2^2$ proportional to $\beta(2 - \beta)$, a downward parabola maximised at $\beta = 1$; the deflected variant of~\cite[Slide 15]{frangioni-slides-nonsmooth} inherits the property. We fix $\beta = 1$ so that $\beta_i = \min(\beta, \gamma_i)$ is driven by $\gamma_i$.

\paragraph{$\gamma_{\min} = 0.05$.}
Condition~(3.5) of~\cite[]{dantonio2009deflected} requires $\beta^* > 0$ strictly, which the floor realises with $\beta^* = \gamma_{\min}$. The value inside $(0, 1]$ is then a tradeoff: a small $\gamma_{\min}$ keeps the clip from binding on most steps, so the deflected direction stays close to the unconstrained greedy minimiser $\gamma^*$ of~\eqref{eq:gamma_star} and preserves the locally optimal Polyak step, at the cost of a $1/\sqrt{\gamma_{\min}}$ factor in the rate of Theorem~\ref{thm:dsm_convergence} (equivalently $1/\gamma_{\min}$ in the iteration count); a large $\gamma_{\min}$ shrinks the constant but binds the clip frequently, diluting the memory term in $\mathbf{d}_i$. We adopt $\gamma_{\min} = 0.05$, the smallest value in the sweep $\{0.05, 0.1, 0.2, 0.5\}$ of Chapter~\ref{chap:results} at which the clip activates only on the near-stationarity degenerate steps documented in Section~\ref{sec:dsm_convergence_conditions}.

\paragraph{$\delta_0 = c\,f(\mathbf{w}_0)$ with $c = 0.1$.}
\cite[Lemma~3.8]{dantonio2009deflected} requires only $\delta_0 > 0$; we need a problem-dependent scale that does not presuppose $f^*$. We scale on the objective at the starting point, $\delta_0 = c\,f(\mathbf{w}_0)$. Since $f^*$ is the global minimum, $f(\mathbf{w}_0) \ge f^*$ for any $\mathbf{w}_0$, so the initial target $f_{\mathrm{ref}} - \delta_0$ is a valid overestimate of the residual gap; if it overshoots, the patience mechanism of Section~\ref{sec:dsm_target_level} contracts $\delta$ until the target is feasible. For the default cold start $\mathbf{w}_0 = \mathbf{0}$ this scale is $f(\mathbf{0}) = \tfrac12\norm{\mathbf{y}}_2^2$, an $O(M)$ evaluation that needs no precomputation; a warm start instead pays the $O(MH^2 + H^3)$ to form $\mathbf{w}_{\mathrm{OLS}}$ first.

The multiplier $c$ trades off two failure modes: small $c$ keeps the target $f_{\mathrm{ref}} - \delta$ close to $f_{\mathrm{ref}}$ and $\delta$ barely contracts, while large $c$ pushes the target below $f^*$ so that only the patience trigger drives progress. Following the moderate values of the target-level literature \cite{brannlund1993generalized,goffin1999convergence,bertsekas1999nonlinear} we set $c = 0.1$. Section~\ref{sec:params} confirms robustness across $c \in [0.01, 1]$, and as an abstract check (Figure~\ref{fig:delta0-families}) that this scale stays within a factor of two of the $f^*$-based scale $\delta_0 = c\,f^*$ wherever SGPTL is in fact optimising --- the latter being usable only as a reference, since $f^*$ is unknown in practice.

\paragraph{$\rho = 0.7$.}
\cite[Lemma~3.8]{dantonio2009deflected} admits any $\rho \in (0, 1)$. Tradeoff: $\rho$ close to $1$ contracts $\delta$ slowly, keeping the target too aggressive for a large fraction of the budget; $\rho$ close to $0$ contracts before the algorithm has had time to exploit each target level. The target-level literature~\cite{brannlund1993generalized,goffin1999convergence,bertsekas1999nonlinear},~\cite[Slide 14]{frangioni-slides-nonsmooth} recommends the moderate range $[0.5,\,0.7]$. A smoke-test sweep at $\rho \in \{0.3,\,0.5,\,0.7,\,0.8,\,0.9,\,0.95\}$ on the synthetic instance and on the two real-data ELM matrices places different $\rho$ as the per-instance best, but only $\rho = 0.7$ is within a factor of two of the best on every tested instance.

\paragraph{$R = 1$.}
\cite[Lemma~3.8]{dantonio2009deflected} admits any $R > 0$ and gives no specific value. Tradeoff: $R$ too small contracts $\delta$ on every minor stagnation, wasting potential progress between target updates; $R$ too large makes the sufficient-descent test the sole driver and stalls if the target is structurally infeasible. $R$ should therefore sit on the per-iteration travel scale $\alpha_i\norm{\mathbf{d}_i}$, which under our other defaults on ELM~LASSO with column-normalised $\mathbf{X}$ is of order $10^{-3}$. We adopt $R = 1$, so the travel-distance branch fires every $\mathcal{O}(10^{3})$ stalled iterations; Section~\ref{sec:warm_cold} reports the empirical contraction counts.

\section{Application to the ELM LASSO problem}
\label{sec:dsm_application}

\subsection{Subgradient computation for ELM LASSO}
\label{sec:dsm_subgrad}

Section~\ref{sec:intro_optimality} already gives the subdifferential of the ELM LASSO objective. Algorithm~\ref{alg:dsm} requires a single element $\mathbf{g}_i \in \partial f(\mathbf{w}_i)$ per iteration; we pick the canonical sign-based representative
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dsm_subgrad}
  \mathbf{g} = \mathbf{X}^\top(\mathbf{X}\mathbf{w} - \mathbf{y}) + \lambda_{\text{LASSO}}\, \mathbf{s},
  \qquad s_i = \mathrm{sign}(w_i) \in \partial|w_i|,
\end{equation}
with the convention $\mathrm{sign}(0) = 0$, which is admissible since $0 \in [-1,1] = \partial|0|$. This choice is not the minimum-norm element of $\partial f(\mathbf{w}_i)$, but Theorem~\ref{thm:dsm_convergence} only requires admissibility. The event $(\mathbf{w}_i)_j = 0$ exactly is non-generic for a subgradient method --- iterates are updated continuously by $\mathbf{w}_{i+1} = \mathbf{w}_i - \alpha_i \mathbf{d}_i$ and never land on a coordinate hyperplane unless $\mathbf{w}_0$ is placed there to begin with; the cold start $\mathbf{w}_0 = \mathbf{0}$ is the only case where the convention is exercised, and only at $i = 0$.

% ------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Convergence analysis and hypothesis verification for ELM LASSO}
\label{sec:dsm_convergence}

\begin{theorem}[Convergence of SGPTL, adapted from {\cite[Slides 14--15]{frangioni-slides-nonsmooth}},~\cite{dantonio2009deflected}]
\label{thm:dsm_convergence}
Let $f : \R^H \to \R$ be convex with $f^* = \min_{\mathbf{w}} f(\mathbf{w}) > -\infty$ attained at some $\mathbf{w}^*$, and let $\{\mathbf{w}_i\}$ be the sequence generated by Algorithm~\ref{alg:dsm}. Assume the subgradients encountered along the run are uniformly bounded, $\norm{\mathbf{g}_i}_2 \leq L$, and the stepsize-restricted rule $\beta_i \le \gamma_i$ holds with $\gamma_i \ge \gamma_{\min} > 0$. Then the record values $\bar{f}_i = \min_{h \le i} f(\mathbf{w}_h)$ converge to $f^*$, and once the target-level estimate has sharpened to $f^*$ (i.e.\ $\delta_k \to 0$ and $f^k_{\mathrm{ref}} \to f^*$, which Lemma~3.8 of \cite{dantonio2009deflected} guarantees) the asymptotic rate, derived below from the per-step Polyak inequality for deflected directions \cite[Slide 15]{frangioni-slides-nonsmooth}, is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:def-rate-deflected}
\bar{f}_k - f^* \;\leq\; \frac{L\,\norm{\mathbf{w}_0 - \mathbf{w}^*}_2}{\sqrt{\gamma_{\min}\,(k+1)}}\,,
\end{equation}
so the worst-case complexity to attain $\bar f_k - f^* \le \varepsilon$ is $O\!\left(L^2/(\gamma_{\min}\,\varepsilon^2)\right)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
We verify the hypotheses of \cite[Theorem~3.7]{dantonio2009deflected} on Algorithm~\ref{alg:dsm} and read off the conclusions. The framework of~\cite{dantonio2009deflected} is stated for $\sigma_k$-inexact subgradients, where $\sigma_k \ge 0$ bounds the oracle error; we compute $\mathbf{g}_i$ exactly via~\eqref{eq:dsm_subgrad}, so the inexactness parameter is zero. The remaining notation maps to ours via $\alpha_k \leftrightarrow \gamma_i$, $\beta_k \leftrightarrow \beta_i$, $\nu_k \leftrightarrow \alpha_i$, $\mu \leftrightarrow \rho$, $X = \R^H$ (no constraint), and $\lambda_k \leftrightarrow f(\mathbf{w}_i) - (f^k_{\mathrm{ref}} - \delta_k)$.

\emph{(a)~Deflection-consistency condition~(2.13).} The condition of \cite[Lemma~2.4]{dantonio2009deflected} requires $v_{k+1} = \tilde d_k$ whenever $d_k = \tilde d_k$, where $\tilde d_k$ is the unprojected deflected direction. In our unconstrained setting $X = \R^H$ no projection occurs, so $d_k = \tilde d_k$ at every iteration and Algorithm~\ref{alg:dsm} sets $v_{k+1} = d_k$, so~(2.13) holds trivially.

\emph{(b)~Stepsize condition~(3.5) of \cite{dantonio2009deflected}.} Translated through the notation mapping above ($\alpha_k \leftrightarrow \gamma_i$, $\beta_k \leftrightarrow \beta_i$), the condition has two clauses:
\begin{itemize}
\item if $\lambda_k \ge 0$ then $\gamma_i \ge \beta_i \ge \beta^* > 0$ for some uniform constant $\beta^* > 0$ independent of $i$;
\item if $\lambda_k < 0$ then the step is null, $\alpha_i = 0$.
\end{itemize}

\emph{Case $\lambda_k > 0$ (target not yet reached).} Line~12 of Algorithm~\ref{alg:dsm} sets $\beta_i = \min(1, \gamma_i)$, and the clip~\eqref{eq:gamma_star} keeps $\gamma_i \in [\gamma_{\min}, 1]$. Hence $\beta_i \ge \gamma_{\min}$ for every $i$, and the uniform lower bound is satisfied with $\beta^* = \gamma_{\min}$. The other inequality $\gamma_i \ge \beta_i$ is just $\gamma_i \ge \min(1, \gamma_i)$, which holds by definition of $\min$.

\emph{Case $\lambda_k \le 0$ (target already attained at $\mathbf{w}_i$).} The numerical safeguard of Section~\ref{sec:dsm_algorithm} skips the iteration with $\alpha_i = 0$, which is the second clause verbatim.

\emph{(c)~Target-level threshold condition~(3.26) of \cite{dantonio2009deflected}.} The condition requires \emph{either} $f^\infty_{\mathrm{ref}} = f^* = -\infty$ \emph{or} both $\liminf\limits_{k\to\infty} \delta_k = 0$ and the series condition~(3.25) of \cite{dantonio2009deflected}, $\sum_k \lambda_k/\norm{\mathbf{d}_k}^2 = \infty$. Since on ELM~LASSO $f^* > -\infty$, the first branch is excluded and we need the second. \cite[Lemma~3.8]{dantonio2009deflected} provides a concrete update strategy for $(f^k_{\mathrm{ref}}, \delta_k)$ --- the sufficient-descent test on $f_{k+1} \le f^k_{\mathrm{ref}} - \delta_k/2$, the target-infeasibility test on $r_k > R$, and the accumulate-$r$ counter --- that delivers both $\liminf\limits_{k\to\infty} \delta_k = 0$ and~(3.25). Lines~16--21 of Algorithm~\ref{alg:dsm} implement that strategy verbatim under the identification $\mu \leftrightarrow \rho$, so condition~(3.26) holds.

\emph{Subgradients uniformly bounded.} The convex objective $f$ of ELM~LASSO is coercive and continuous; the iterates $\{\mathbf{w}_i\}$ remain in a compact sublevel set $S_{c_0}$ for some $c_0 \ge f(\mathbf{w}_0)$, and $\partial f$ is uniformly bounded on $S_{c_0}$ by Lipschitz continuity of convex functions on compact sets \cite[Slide 9]{frangioni-slides-smooth}.

Conditions (2.13), (3.5), (3.26) of~\cite{dantonio2009deflected} being verified with $\sigma^* = 0$, \cite[Theorem~3.7]{dantonio2009deflected} gives $f^\infty_{\mathrm{ref}} \le f^* + \sigma^* = f^*$, so $\bar f_i \to f^*$. To attach a rate to this convergence we work in the regime where the target-level update has driven $f^k_{\mathrm{ref}} - \delta_k$ to $f^*$ (Lemma~3.8 of~\cite{dantonio2009deflected}); the Polyak step at iteration $i$ then uses target $f^*$ exactly, $\alpha_i = \beta_i(f(\mathbf{w}_i) - f^*)/\norm{\mathbf{d}_i}_2^2$.

\emph{Per-step descent.} The stepsize-restricted Polyak inequality for a deflected direction~\cite[Slide 15]{frangioni-slides-nonsmooth} controls the squared distance to the optimum:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:def-step-polyak}
\norm{\mathbf{w}_{i+1} - \mathbf{w}^*}_2^2 \;\le\; \norm{\mathbf{w}_i - \mathbf{w}^*}_2^2 \;-\; \beta_i\,(2 - \beta_i)\,\frac{(f(\mathbf{w}_i) - f^*)^2}{\norm{\mathbf{d}_i}_2^2}.
\end{equation}
Algorithm~\ref{alg:dsm} sets $\beta_i = \min(1, \gamma_i)$ with $\gamma_i \in [\gamma_{\min}, 1]$, so $\beta_i = \gamma_i$ and $\beta_i(2 - \beta_i) = \gamma_i(2 - \gamma_i)$. On $[\gamma_{\min}, 1]$ the function $\gamma \mapsto \gamma(2 - \gamma)$ is increasing, so it is bounded below by $\gamma_{\min}(2 - \gamma_{\min}) \ge \gamma_{\min}$ (using $\gamma_{\min} \le 1$).

\emph{Telescoping.} Summing~\eqref{eq:def-step-polyak} from $i = 0$ to $i = k$ and dropping the non-negative left-hand side at index $k+1$,
\[
\gamma_{\min} \sum_{i=0}^{k} \frac{(f(\mathbf{w}_i) - f^*)^2}{\norm{\mathbf{d}_i}_2^2} \;\le\; \norm{\mathbf{w}_0 - \mathbf{w}^*}_2^2.
\]
Each $\mathbf{d}_i$ is a convex combination of subgradients along the run, so $\norm{\mathbf{d}_i}_2 \le \max_{h \le i}\norm{\mathbf{g}_h}_2 \le L$; substituting,
\[
\sum_{i=0}^{k} (f(\mathbf{w}_i) - f^*)^2 \;\le\; \frac{L^2\,\norm{\mathbf{w}_0 - \mathbf{w}^*}_2^2}{\gamma_{\min}}.
\]
The record value satisfies $\bar f_k - f^* = \min_{h \le k}(f(\mathbf{w}_h) - f^*) \le f(\mathbf{w}_i) - f^*$ for every $i \le k$, so $(k+1)(\bar f_k - f^*)^2$ lower-bounds the sum, giving~\eqref{eq:def-rate-deflected}.

\emph{Iteration count.} Solving the right-hand side of~\eqref{eq:def-rate-deflected} for $\varepsilon$ yields
\[
  k + 1 \;\ge\; \frac{L^2\,\norm{\mathbf{w}_0 - \mathbf{w}^*}_2^2}{\gamma_{\min}\,\varepsilon^2}.
\]
The unfloored Polyak bound (same derivation with $\gamma_{\min}$ replaced by $1$) is $k+1 \ge L^2\norm{\mathbf{w}_0 - \mathbf{w}^*}_2^2/\varepsilon^2$, so the deflection floor inflates the iteration count by the factor $1/\gamma_{\min}$. With $\gamma_{\min} = 0.05$ this is a $20\times$ multiplier; the rate order $O(L^2/\varepsilon^2)$ is unchanged.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Verification of theorem assumptions for ELM LASSO.}
Convexity, coercivity, and uniqueness of $\mathbf{w}^*$ under full column rank of $\mathbf{X}$ follow from Proposition~\ref{prop:irls_prop}. The subgradient bound used in the proof requires only that $f$ is Lipschitz on the iterate sublevel set, not globally: the smooth part $\mathbf{X}^\top(\mathbf{X}\mathbf{w} - \mathbf{y})$ grows with $\norm{\mathbf{w}}$, but $\{\mathbf{w}_i\}$ stays in a compact set (\cite[Theorem~3.7]{dantonio2009deflected}), so the local Lipschitz constant $L$ in~\eqref{eq:def-rate-deflected} is finite.

\paragraph{Complexity.}
The lower bound for first-order methods on nonsmooth convex problems is $\Omega(L^2/\varepsilon^2)$ \cite[Slide 8]{frangioni-slides-nonsmooth},~\cite{bubeck2015convex}; Algorithm~\ref{alg:dsm} attains it. Deflection improves constants, not the order.

\section{Expected practical behavior}
\label{sec:dsm_properties}

Theorem~\ref{thm:dsm_convergence} establishes that the record sequence $\bar f_i = \min_{h \le i} f(\mathbf{w}_h)$ converges to $f^*$ at the rate $\bar f_k - f^* \lesssim L\norm{\mathbf{w}_0 - \mathbf{w}^*}_2 / \sqrt{\gamma_{\min}\,(k+1)}$ of~\eqref{eq:def-rate-deflected}. From execution we therefore expect a concave curve of $\bar f_i - f^*$ on a semilog plot, with the residual gap closing as $1/\sqrt{i}$. The theorem bounds the record, not the raw values $f(\mathbf{w}_i)$, so we expect $f(\mathbf{w}_i)$ to oscillate around $\bar f_i$ rather than decrease step by step.

The same bound depends on two ingredients which are problem-specific. The Lipschitz constant $L$ scales with $\norm{\mathbf{X}}_2$ and $\lambda_{\text{LASSO}}$ (verification of theorem assumptions, Section~\ref{sec:dsm_convergence}); from~\eqref{eq:def-rate-deflected} we therefore expect larger $\lambda_{\text{LASSO}}$ to inflate $L$ and shift the entire curve upward. The initial distance $\norm{\mathbf{w}_0 - \mathbf{w}^*}_2$ enters multiplicatively, and this is what separates the two starts. The cold start $\mathbf{w}_0 = \mathbf{0}$ gives $\norm{\mathbf{w}_0 - \mathbf{w}^*}_2 = \norm{\mathbf{w}^*}_2$ exactly, so~\eqref{eq:def-rate-deflected} predicts a higher curve; the OLS warm start gives $\norm{\mathbf{w}_{\mathrm{OLS}} - \mathbf{w}^*}_2$, which is small whenever OLS is already close to the LASSO minimiser --- the two coincide at $\lambda_{\text{LASSO}} = 0$ and vary continuously with it --- predicting a lower starting gap. How wide the resulting gap between the two starts is therefore depends on the instance: on a well-conditioned design where $\mathbf{w}_{\mathrm{OLS}}$ sits near $\mathbf{w}^*$ the warm start should begin well below the cold one, whereas where $\mathbf{w}_{\mathrm{OLS}}$ is far from $\mathbf{w}^*$ the two should be comparable. We run both starts on every dataset and report the respective analysis (Section~\ref{sec:warm_cold}).

The rate~\eqref{eq:def-rate-deflected} controls $\bar f_i - f^*$, not the magnitude of individual coordinates of $\mathbf{w}_i$. A subgradient step $\mathbf{w}_{i+1} = \mathbf{w}_i - \alpha_i \mathbf{d}_i$ updates each coordinate continuously and never enforces $(\mathbf{w}_i)_j = 0$ exactly; combined with the rate~\eqref{eq:def-rate-deflected}, this leaves an inactive component at a magnitude no larger than the residual scale of the rate, $O\!\bigl(L/\sqrt{\gamma_{\min}\,i}\bigr)$. We therefore do not expect SGPTL to produce hard sparsity at termination; recovery of the support will require a thresholding step set above this residual scale.

The optimality condition $0 \in \partial f(\mathbf{w}^*)$ of Section~\ref{sec:intro_optimality} is a set condition that holds \emph{at} $\mathbf{w}^*$; it asks for some element of the set $\partial f(\mathbf{w}^*)$ to vanish, not for any element of $\partial f(\mathbf{w}_i)$ at a generic iterate. Under the sublinear rate~\eqref{eq:def-rate-deflected} the iterates do not reach $\mathbf{w}^*$ within a finite budget, so we do not expect $\norm{\mathbf{g}_i}_2 \to 0$ along the run regardless of which subdifferential representative is computed in~\eqref{eq:dsm_subgrad}. Termination will therefore rely on $i_{\max}$ or on a flat-window test on $\bar f_i$ rather than on a subgradient-based optimality check.
