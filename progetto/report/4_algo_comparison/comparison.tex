\chapter{Algorithm Comparison}
\label{chap:comparison}

In this chapter, we will compare the two algorithms together to analyze their properties and trade-offs.

% ------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Core strategy: how each algorithm handles non-smoothness}
\label{sec:comp_strategy}

\paragraph{IRLS: smoothing via majorization}\mbox{} \\
IRLS replaces $f$ with a smooth quadratic majorant $Q(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{w}_k)$ (\ref{sec:irls_mm}), reducing each step to a
weighted $L_2$ problem solved in closed form via the normal equations
(\ref{sec:irls_subproblem}).

\paragraph{DSM: direct subgradient with memory}\mbox{} \\
DSM descends along a subgradient $\mathbf{g} \in \partial f(\mathbf{w}_i)$
(\ref{sec:dsm_subgrad}), deflected with the previous direction
(\ref{sec:dsm_deflection}) with Polyak step using
a self-adapting target estimate of $f^*$ (\ref{sec:dsm_target_level}).

% ------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Convergence behaviour}
\label{sec:comp_convergence}

\subsection{Monotonicity}

The majorization-minimization construction of Section~\ref{sec:irls_mm} guarantees IRLS is \textbf{monotone}: $f(\mathbf{w}_{k+1}) \leq f(\mathbf{w}_k)$. DSM is
\textbf{non-monotone}: only the record $\bar{f}^{\,i} = \min_{h \leq i} f(\mathbf{w}_h)$
is monotone, so DSM returns $\mathbf{w}_{\mathrm{best}}$ and stops on a fixed budget.

\subsection{Rate}

IRLS achieves a \textbf{linear} rate (\ref{sec:irls_convergence}); DSM achieves the optimal \textbf{sublinear} $O(L^2/\varepsilon^2)$ rate for nonsmooth convex functions (Theorem~\ref{thm:dsm_convergence}). Let's analyze the behaviour when we lower $\varepsilon$:
\begin{itemize}
  \item DSM: $\bar f_k - f^* \lesssim L\norm{\mathbf{w}_0 - \mathbf{w}^*}_2/(\gamma_{\min}\sqrt k)$ gives $k \propto 1/\varepsilon^2$. Lowering $\varepsilon$ by a factor $10$ therefore multiplies the iteration count by $10^2 = 100$.
  \item IRLS: $f(\mathbf{w}_{k+1}) - f^* \le r\,(f(\mathbf{w}_k) - f^*)$ with $r \in (0,1)$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:irls_convergence}) gives $k \propto \log(1/\varepsilon)/\log(1/r)$. Lowering $\varepsilon$ by a factor $10$ \emph{adds} the constant $\log 10/\log(1/r)$ iterations, independent of $\varepsilon$.
\end{itemize}
For more precision of the target solution, DSM requires a polynomial number of additional steps, meanwhile IRLS requires only logarithmic (additively constant per decade).

\subsection{Convergence on the ELM problem}

As expressed before, $\mathbf{X}$ has full column rank, so $f$ is $\mu$-strongly convex with $\mu = \lambda_{\min}(\mathbf{X}^\top\mathbf{X}) > 0$ and admits a unique minimizer $\mathbf{w}^*$ (\ref{prop:irls_prop}). Both algorithms are theoretically guaranteed to converge on this problem, but with different notions of convergence: Theorem~\ref{thm:irls_convergence} gives IRLS iterates $\mathbf{w}_k \to \mathbf{w}^*$ at a linear rate, while Theorem~\ref{thm:dsm_convergence} gives SGPTL only the weaker guarantee $\bar f_i \to f^*$ on the record sequence $\bar f_i = \min_{h \le i} f(\mathbf{w}_h)$, but single iterates $\{\mathbf{w}_i\}$ may oscillate.

\section{Computational cost}
\label{sec:comp_cost}

\subsection{Per-iteration cost}

\begin{table}[htbp]
\centering
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\toprule
\textbf{Operation} & \textbf{IRLS} & \textbf{DSM} \\
\midrule
Weight / direction update & $O(H)$ & $O(H)$ \\
Matrix-vector product(s) & --- & $O(MH)$ \\
Linear system solve & $O(H^3)$ (Cholesky) or $O(pH^2)$ (CG) & --- \\
\midrule
\textbf{Total per iteration} & $O(H^3)$ & $O(MH)$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{Per-iteration computational cost of IRLS and DSM.}
\label{tab:comp_cost}
\end{table}

IRLS solves an $H \times H$ SPD system per iteration
(\ref{sec:irls_subproblem}): $O(H^3/3)$ via Cholesky, or $O(pH^2)$
via $p$ CG steps when $H$ is large and $\mathbf{Q}_k$ well-conditioned.

DSM uses two matrix-vector products per iteration ($\mathbf{X}\mathbf{w}_i$
and $\mathbf{X}^T(\cdot)$, each $O(MH)$) (\ref{sec:dsm_subgrad}).

\subsection{Total cost}

Accounting for both the precomputation phase and the iteration loop, the total costs are
\begin{align*}
    \text{IRLS (OLS warm start):}                 &\quad {O(MH^2) + O(MH) + O(H^3)} + k_{\mathrm{IRLS}} \cdot O(H^3) \\
    \text{IRLS (cold start):}                 &\quad {O(MH^2) + O(MH)} + k_{\mathrm{IRLS}} \cdot O(H^3) \\
    \text{SGPTL with OLS warm start:}             &\quad O(MH^2) + O(H^3) + k_{\mathrm{DSM}} \cdot O(MH), \\
    \text{SGPTL with cold start $\mathbf{w}_0 = \mathbf{0}$:}\;\;\; &\quad k_{\mathrm{DSM}} \cdot O(MH).
\end{align*}

On the ELM problem, $M \gg H$ is typical, so $O(MH^2)$ dominates IRLS's total. IRLS therefore wins whenever $k_{\mathrm{DSM}} \gtrsim H$: the warm-start variant of SGPTL already pays the same $O(MH^2)$ overhead and adds the iteration cost on top, while the cold-start variant trades the overhead for $k_{\mathrm{DSM}}$ matrix-vector products at $O(MH)$ each. Since Theorem~\ref{thm:dsm_convergence} forces $k_{\mathrm{DSM}} = O(L^2/\varepsilon^2)$ with $L$ growing in $H$ (the $\ell_1$ subgradient has norm $\le \lambda_{\text{LASSO}}\sqrt{H}$), the inequality $k_{\mathrm{DSM}} > H$ holds at every $H$ we test for any reasonable accuracy.

\section{Solution quality}
\label{sec:comp_quality}

\subsection{Sparsity}

IRLS yields numerically sparse iterates whenever we have small components of $\mathbf{w}$, which are sent towards $\varepsilon$ (theoretically $0$) (\ref{sec:irls_expected}). DSM gives only
\textbf{approximate sparsity}; an explicit thresholding step is needed
(\ref{sec:dsm_properties}).

\subsection{Accuracy}

At equal budget, IRLS reaches much higher accuracy on small-to-moderate
problems thanks to its linear rate. DSM is competitive only when
$H^2 \gg M$ (Table~\ref{tab:comp_cost}).

\section{Comparison summary for the ELM problem}
\label{sec:comp_summary}

\begin{table}[htbp]
\centering
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\toprule
\textbf{Criterion} & \textbf{IRLS} & \textbf{DSM} \\
\midrule
Approach & Smoothing (MM) & Direct subgradient \\
Monotone & Yes & No (record value only) \\
Convergence rate & Linear & Sublinear $O(L^2/\varepsilon^2)$ \\
Per-iteration cost & $O(H^3)$ & $O(MH)$ \\
Precomputation & ${O(MH^2) + O(MH) + O(H^3)}$ & None \\
Exact sparsity & Yes & No (needs thresholding) \\
Parameters to tune & $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{thr}}$ & $\delta_0, \rho, R, \beta$ \\
Stopping criterion & Reliable (iterate change) & Unreliable (fixed budget) \\
Preferred when & High accuracy, $H \ll M$ & Large $H$, moderate accuracy \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{Summary comparison of IRLS and DSM on the ELM LASSO problem.}
\label{tab:comp_summary}
\end{table}

Making the assumption that $M \gg H$, IRLS algorithm is preferred: the $O(MH^2)$ precomputation of $\mathbf{A}$ amortizes over a few cheap Cholesky solves, inducing sparsity, while only having to tune $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{thr}}$. DSM is the better choice only when $H^2 \gg M$ or $\mathbf{X}^T\mathbf{X}$ does not fit in memory, and the accuracy target is moderate ($\varepsilon \sim 10^{-3}$).
